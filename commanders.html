new mystery booster commanders

The eight new Mystery Booster Rulebreaker commanders are Seluma, Tolabow, The Unluckiest Planeswalker, Maular, Grizzlegom, Whtz, The Everforger, and Valko Indorian. Their shared feature is a specific deck-construction exception: most expand the color identity of selected card categories, while Whtz changes the maximum deck size. [Card text]

Seluma, Light of Aysen

Collector number
#4 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{4}{W} · White [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Creature — Angel Warrior · 4/4 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

Angel cards may have any color identity, and the deck may include any basic land cards. [Card text]

Other abilities

Flying; combat damage to a player lets Seluma return a target Angel creature card from your graveyard to the battlefield. [Card text]

Deck direction

Angel-focused combat with graveyard recovery.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that Seluma asks for loyalty to a creature theme instead of loyalty to a single color. Opening the Angel slot makes that theme the organizing principle, while the combat reward gives the deck a reason to put its commander at risk. I would build around a coherent Angel plan rather than treat the extra colors as permission to collect unrelated powerful cards.

Tolabow, Loch Rascal

Collector number
#12 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{2}{U}{U} · Blue [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Creature — Otter · 3/2 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

Choose one color outside the commander's color identity. Instant and sorcery cards may include that chosen color alongside blue, and the deck may include any basic land cards. This does not open the other card types to that color. [Card text]

Other abilities

Each instant or sorcery you cast makes a 1/1 blue and red Otter creature token with prowess. [Card text]

Deck direction

Instants and sorceries that build an Otter board, with one carefully chosen extra spell color.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that Tolabow makes the additional color a deliberate tool choice rather than a full second-color package. Only the instant-and-sorcery portion expands, and those spells also supply the creature board. I would choose the extra color for what it adds to the spell plan, then keep the rest of the list focused on making repeated spell casts useful. The blue-red token colors should not be mistaken for a red-only deckbuilding permission.

The Unluckiest Planeswalker

Collector number
#26 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{2}{R} · Red [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Planeswalker · Starting loyalty 4 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

Aura cards may have any color identity, and the deck may include any basic land cards. [Card text]

Other abilities

Its +1 supplies mana and can add a Gremlin when that mana casts an Aura. Its −3 trades your hand for cards based on your Auras and moves Auras attached to you onto opponents. [Card text]

Deck direction

An Aura-and-Curse strategy built around its unusual enchantment permissions.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that the narrow Aura permission preserves the character of a red commander while changing the tools around it. The interesting decision is which Auras support a single plan, not how many colors can fit in the list. I would make the Aura package and the loyalty abilities work toward the same outcome instead of treating every off-color Aura as automatically desirable.

Maular, the Next Evolution

Collector number
#31 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{5}{G}{G} · Green [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Creature — Dinosaur Mutant · 10/10 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

Creature cards with mana value 7 or greater may have any color identity, and the deck may include any basic land cards. [Card text]

Other abilities

When one of your creatures with mana value 7 or greater attacks, its power and toughness double until the turn ends. [Card text]

Deck direction

High-mana-value creatures, a supporting mana plan, and attacking.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that Maular makes a high curve a deckbuilding commitment rather than just a collection of finishers. The permission broadens the large-creature choices, but the attack reward still asks the deck to turn those choices into combat. I would prioritize a reliable route to those creatures and a reason for each inclusion, rather than assume that access to more large creatures solves the list by itself.

Legendary Creature — Minotaur Warrior · 4/4 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

The deck may include any land cards; the exception is for lands, not for every card type. [Card text]

Deck direction

A red-green lands strategy that develops basic land types and attacks with its commander.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that Grizzlegom moves the most interesting deckbuilding decision into the mana base. The lands are both infrastructure and the source of the attack rewards, so choosing them becomes part of the deck's identity. I would balance useful land types against the need to cast the red-green commander. Access to every land is an invitation to plan the mana base, not a requirement to make it as elaborate as possible.

Whtz, the Bibliophile

Collector number
#57 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{1}{W}{U} · White and blue [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Creature — Homunculus · 1/3 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

The deck has no maximum deck size. This exception changes deck size, not color identity. [Card text]

Other abilities

{3}, {T}: draw a card and gain 1 life. If your starting deck contained 200 or more cards, the activation costs {3} less; the tap cost remains. [Card text]

Deck direction

A deliberately oversized white-blue deck built to benefit from the 200-card starting-deck threshold.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that Whtz turns a deckbuilding disadvantage into a resource decision. A larger list makes it harder to rely on one particular draw, so the reward gives the player a reason to accept that dilution. I would emphasize cards with overlapping jobs and a clear plan for using the draw ability. This is the exception that shows why Rulebreaker is broader than simply allowing extra colors.

The Everforger

Collector number
#59 [Card text]
Mana cost / color
{6} · Colorless [Card text]
Type / stats
Legendary Artifact Creature — Construct · 4/5 [Card text]

Rulebreaker — paraphrased

Artifact creature and Equipment cards may have any color identity, and the deck may include any basic land cards. [Card text]

Other abilities

Once each turn, you may copy an artifact creature or Equipment spell you cast; the copy becomes a token. [Card text]

Deck direction

Artifact creatures and Equipment, with deliberate choices about which eligible spell to copy each turn.

Why this design matters — our reading

My reading is that The Everforger makes card category the organizing principle for a colorless commander. Its broad selection is checked by a narrow payoff: the list still needs artifact creatures or Equipment worth copying. I would choose a coherent mix of bodies and Equipment rather than read the permission as access to every artifact. The once-per-turn limit also makes sequencing part of the build's identity.

One mechanic, different boundaries

What these commanders have in common

These eight commanders use Rulebreaker to change a stated deck-construction restriction. The lineup has five monocolored commanders, two two-color commanders, and one colorless commander. [Card text] [Set reference]

The color identity exceptions are category-specific: Angels for Seluma, a chosen extra spell color for Tolabow, Auras for The Unluckiest Planeswalker, large creatures for Maular, lands for Grizzlegom, artifact creatures and Equipment for The Everforger, and Phyrexians for Valko. Whtz is different: its exception removes the maximum deck size rather than opening extra colors. [Card text]

Rulebreaker applies when the card is your designated commander and changes only the restrictions its text mentions. It is not a blanket permission to use any card of any color. [Card text]

The Commander Format Panel participated in the Rulebreaker design. [Set reference]

Our reading: the mechanic makes a commander's theme a deckbuilding boundary in its own right. The useful question is which exception advances the deck's plan, rather than how many colors can be added.
